# GLAZE UI V1.0-Origin — Application and Service Icon Identity

**Contract origin:** Official V1.0 reset baseline; inherited by later V1 releases unless superseded.  
**Current Stable product authority:** GLAZE UI V1.2 / `1.2.0`.

`tokens/icon-identity.json` retains an internal icon-identity contract revision and baseline of `1.5.0`. That value is a subsystem-contract revision only. It does not define another Glaze UI product release, consumer target, or production-acceptance state. Product lifecycle authority remains separate in `registry/lifecycle.json` and `VERSION`.

## Governing principle

The central identity rule is **shared DNA without shared identity**. The V1 identity grammar supplies common ecosystem structure while each product retains a distinct, memorable identity. This contract originated under GLAZE UI V1.0 and remains inherited by the current V1.2 Stable product where not superseded.

## Identity hierarchy

Every first-party product identity is evaluated across four layers:

**Ecosystem → Family → Product → State**

Ecosystem establishes GoreeCloud and Glaze coherence. Family establishes a restrained relationship to related products. Product carries the unique identity users recognize. State remains temporary and must not replace, recolor away, or otherwise destroy the stable product identity.

## Identity DNA and Identity Lock

Every major application and service requires an **Identity DNA** profile. The profile may record primary symbol, silhouette, geometric motif, directional structure, characteristic negative space, material personality, identity color family, secondary accent, depth behavior, and motion personality.

Identity DNA complements the Identity Lock. DNA describes broader personality and family relationships; the Identity Lock records recognition properties that must survive adaptive presentation, accessibility treatment, optical-size reduction, and redesign.

## Ecosystem and family rules

The GoreeCloud ecosystem is one visual family, but product families may establish restrained secondary relationships. Family resemblance may come from material behavior, compositional rhythm, secondary geometry, lighting, depth, or motion personality. Family membership must never be implemented as the same symbol with different colors.

The governed first-party family registry includes communication, productivity, media, cloud-and-data, network, security, system-utility, AI-and-intelligence, browser-and-discovery, and persistence-and-resilience. Family metadata must not flatten cross-domain products into a single stereotype.

## Product color ownership

Each major product owns an identity color family rather than one literal color. Color is secondary to shape: neighboring products must remain distinguishable in monochrome, and branding must not appropriate protected semantic colors for success, information, warning, danger, privacy, security, protected, restricted, online, offline, syncing, unavailable, or other system states.

## Shape and negative-space ownership

Distinctive arrangements of geometry and negative space may become registered identity traits. Common primitives remain reusable, but neighboring products must not casually duplicate distinctive silhouettes, cutouts, gap relationships, or characteristic arrangements when doing so would create confusion.

## Material, depth, and motion personality

Products may express different personalities only within the inherited V1 material, depth, accessibility, and motion grammar. Motion is allowed only when it communicates meaningful state, and Reduced Motion behavior is mandatory.

## Service derivation

Associated services derive relationship from an application's Identity DNA without copying the application icon. A service may inherit a geometric motif, identity-color relationship, or symbolic fragment while using the simpler service construction hierarchy.

Parent and child services may share a structural motif while child services add capability-specific geometry. Background services prioritize capability recognition over branding. Low-level infrastructure components use reduced symbolic representation while retaining Glaze geometry, semantic color, and accessibility rules.

Temporary service states remain separate overlays or adjacent treatments. Running, stopped, degraded, updating, syncing, offline, warning, and failed states must not mutate the underlying service identity.

## Third-party and legacy identities

Third-party identities remain third-party identities. Glaze UI may normalize their presentation through framing, padding, masks, environmental response, badges, selection, contrast, or accessibility treatment, but it must not impose GoreeCloud family motifs or falsely rebrand the asset.

Legacy identities may use an adaptive normalization surface where necessary. Preserving recognition is preferred over aggressive restyling.

## Identity review

Every major first-party identity is reviewed against the ecosystem, its primary family, neighboring products, associated services, monochrome presentation, optical-size reduction, accessibility modes, and expected state overlays.

The review vocabulary includes recognition, distinctiveness, family relationship, Glaze coherence, scalability, accessibility, and longevity. Automated signals support human review; a single automated score is never authoritative.

## Collision detection

Collision detection is a review aid, not an autonomous rejection authority. Tooling may flag similarities in silhouette, dominant geometry, negative space, color distribution, or composition. Human review determines whether the similarity creates real user confusion.

## Ecosystem Wall and family preview

Family previews and an **Ecosystem Wall** may be produced with ordinary design tools, generated contact sheets, repository previews, or equivalent review artifacts. They should show major application and service identities at multiple optical sizes and reveal repetitive geometry, color clustering, visual-weight imbalance, weak identities, and accidental hierarchy.

Glaze UI does not require or plan a dedicated Icon Studio application. Automated collision analysis and Ecosystem Wall generation remain Planned where their separate implementation would materially improve review quality; human review remains authoritative.

## New product creation and redesign

New product identity work begins with meaning, differentiation, family relationship, reserved concepts, and a proposed long-term Identity Lock before rendering exploration begins. Recognition precedes rendering polish. Beauty should emerge from structure, proportion, negative space, material relationships, light, and restrained color rather than decorative complexity.

Existing identities should not be replaced merely because visual trends change. Evolve presentation first when the Identity Lock remains effective. Major replacement is reserved for identities that are confusing, inaccessible, excessively generic, technically limiting, or no longer representative.

## Application-to-service relationship

Application icons communicate destination and product identity. Service icons communicate capability and infrastructure. Shared DNA communicates ownership; different construction communicates role.

## Final identity questions

A successful identity should answer four questions quickly: What is this? What family does it belong to? What state is it in? Does it belong to Glaze UI?

## V1 inheritance and Stable promotion boundary

This contract originated in the official GLAZE UI V1.0 reset baseline, was inherited by V1.1, and remains inherited by V1.2 where not superseded. The internal `1.5.0` icon-identity revision remains a subsystem revision and must not be interpreted as a Glaze UI product version. Source validation proves contract consistency only. It does **not** establish production Stable acceptance for an icon implementation. Production acceptance requires fresh exact-revision ecosystem review, family review, monochrome and optical-size validation, accessibility review, state-overlay review, collision assessment, continuity assessment where applicable, and confirmation that Planned review automation is not represented as implemented.
